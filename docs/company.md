# Bakersfield Dirt

Bakersfield Dirt sells bulk materials and arranges trucking for projects in Bakersfield and Kern County. Its specialty is moving dirt in both directions. A contractor building up a site needs suitable fill brought in. A contractor cutting a site down may need someone to haul excess dirt out. When the material, locations and schedules fit, Bakersfield Dirt can connect the two jobs.

Customers can also buy base rock, rock, gravel, sand, asphalt and landscaping materials and arrange delivery through the same company. A buyer who already has a material source can ask about trucking separately. The website should make all three inquiries easy to start.

## What the business brings to a job

The existing site identifies developers, contractors and builders as customers, with work ranging from residential grading to infrastructure projects. It describes a network of material sites and local subhaulers, names transfer trucks, Super-10s, bottom dumps and end dumps, and connects the business to the Bowman family's construction background. These are useful details for About. A short account of how the team finds a source, a receiving site and a suitable truck will tell a customer more than a list of company values. ## One Services page, organized by the job

Use a short index at the top. A customer looking for gravel should be able to jump straight to it, while someone with a pile of dirt should immediately see where to request removal.

| Section | What it should explain |
| --- | --- |
| **Dirt delivery (import)** · `#dirt-delivery` | Bring fill onto a site. Lead with clean, compactable fill, the dirt the current site identifies. Ask where it is going, roughly how much is needed and whether the job specifies a material. |
| **Dirt haul-away (export)** · `#dirt-haul-away` | Move surplus dirt off a site. Explain how the team checks the material and looks for a suitable receiving site or a nearby fill need. Ask where the pile is, what it contains and how trucks can reach it. |
| **Materials for sale** · `#materials` | Give each family below a visible heading. For each, explain a typical use, what the buyer should specify and how to ask for delivered pricing. |
| **Trucking & hauling** · `#trucking` | Cover delivery with a material purchase and haul-only inquiries. Pickup and drop-off locations, site access and loading conditions help determine which truck fits. |

### Materials within the catalog

| Buyer-facing heading | Useful distinction for the buyer |
| --- | --- |
| **Fill dirt** | Link to dirt delivery for quantities, destination and project requirements. People looking for a place to take surplus fill have a separate haul-away link. |
| **Base rock** | For a compacted base under a road, driveway or pad, ask for the base specification shown on the plans. Keep “base” and “base rock” together. |
| **Rock & gravel** | Group these under one heading while naming both materials. Ask about intended use, desired size and quantity. |
| **Sand** | Ask what the sand will be used for and whether the buyer has a specified type or gradation. |
| **Asphalt** | Identify the form or specification the job calls for and confirm what can be supplied and hauled. |
| **Landscaping materials** | Let buyers describe the material, finish and quantity they want. Link to gravel or sand when that is what they need. |

“Supplies” describes the catalog as a whole. Named materials give buyers more useful choices. One Services page also lets a buyer compare materials and arrange a truck in the same visit.

## From inquiry to quote

Use “What do you need?” with four choices matching the Services sections. Collect a name, a way to reply, the job location, material, estimated quantity with units, and desired timing. Allow “unsure” for quantity. For a haul, invite pickup and destination details if known. For surplus dirt, invite a description of the dirt and access for loading. The team can then confirm availability, suitability and pricing with the customer.

**Details to settle before publishing sales copy.** Confirm available grades, asphalt products, landscaping inventory, haul-away acceptance criteria and the normal delivery area beyond Kern County. The existing site also mentions grading, paving, leveling and excavation. The redesign brief centers on sales and trucking, so those construction trades need owner confirmation before they appear as bookable services.
